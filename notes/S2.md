# S2 · Rotations: SO(3), quaternions, Euler, slerp

## Core ideas
$R\in SO(3)$: $R^TR=I$, $\det R=+1$. 9 numbers, 6 constraints, **3 DOF**. A reflection has $\det=-1$.
Naming: `R_wb` maps body-frame coordinates to world: $p_w = R_{wb}\,p_b$; $R_{bw}=R_{wb}^T$.

### Exp / log (Rodrigues), $\boldsymbol\omega=\theta\mathbf a$, $K=[\boldsymbol\omega]_\times$
$$\exp(\boldsymbol\omega)=I+\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}K+\frac{1-\cos\theta}{\theta^2}K^2$$
$$\theta=\operatorname{atan2}\!\Big(\tfrac12\lVert\operatorname{vee}(R-R^T)\rVert,\ \tfrac{\operatorname{tr}R-1}{2}\Big),\qquad \boldsymbol\omega=\frac{\theta}{2\sin\theta}\operatorname{vee}(R-R^T)$$
| Regime | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| $\theta\to0$ | $0/0$ | Taylor: $\exp\approx I+K+\tfrac12K^2$; $\log\approx\tfrac12\operatorname{vee}(R-R^T)$ |
| $\theta\to\pi$ | $\sin\theta\to0$, axis lost | $\tfrac12(R+R^T)=\cos\theta\,I+(1-\cos\theta)\,\mathbf a\mathbf a^T$ → largest diagonal column → $\mathbf a$; sign from $\operatorname{vee}(R-R^T)$ |
| rounding | $\tfrac{\operatorname{tr}-1}{2}>1$ → `acos` = NaN | clamp to $[-1,1]$, or use `atan2` |

### Quaternions $q=(w,\mathbf v)=(\cos\tfrac\theta2,\ \mathbf a\sin\tfrac\theta2)$, unit norm, Hamilton convention
$$R(q)=\begin{bmatrix}1-2(y^2+z^2)&2(xy-wz)&2(xz+wy)\\2(xy+wz)&1-2(x^2+z^2)&2(yz-wx)\\2(xz-wy)&2(yz+wx)&1-2(x^2+y^2)\end{bmatrix}$$
**R → q (Shepperd):** $\operatorname{tr}R=4w^2-1$. If tr > 0: $w=\tfrac12\sqrt{1+\operatorname{tr}}$, $x=(R_{21}-R_{12})/4w$, … Otherwise use the largest diagonal entry $R_{ii}$, e.g. $x=\tfrac12\sqrt{1+R_{00}-R_{11}-R_{22}}$, $w=(R_{21}-R_{12})/4x$, $y=(R_{01}+R_{10})/4x$, $z=(R_{02}+R_{20})/4x$.
**Double cover:** $q$ and $-q$ are the same rotation. Canonicalize with $w\ge0$, and align signs before averaging or interpolating.

```cpp
Eigen::Quaterniond q(w, x, y, z);        // ctor order: w first
q.coeffs();                              // storage order: (x, y, z, w)!
Eigen::Quaterniond(R);  q.toRotationMatrix();  q.normalized();  q.conjugate();
Eigen::AngleAxisd(theta, axis.normalized());   // axis MUST be unit
q1.angularDistance(q2);  q1.slerp(t, q2);
R.eulerAngles(2, 1, 0);                  // ZYX, but first angle in [0, pi]: surprising
```

### Euler ZYX (yaw, pitch, roll): $R=R_z(\psi)R_y(\theta)R_x(\phi)$
$\psi=\operatorname{atan2}(R_{10},R_{00})$, $\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(-R_{20},\sqrt{R_{00}^2+R_{10}^2})$, $\phi=\operatorname{atan2}(R_{21},R_{22})$.
**Gimbal lock** at $\theta=\pm90°$: only $\psi\mp\phi$ is observable. Set $\phi=0$ and $\psi=\operatorname{atan2}(-R_{01},R_{11})$.

### Slerp
$$\operatorname{slerp}(a,b,t)=\frac{\sin((1-t)\Omega)}{\sin\Omega}a+\frac{\sin(t\Omega)}{\sin\Omega}b,\quad\cos\Omega=a\cdot b$$
If dot < 0, negate b (shortest path). If dot ≈ 1, fall back to nlerp. Equivalent form: $a\exp(t\log(a^{-1}b))$.

### Nearest rotation (orthogonal Procrustes)
$M=U\Sigma V^T \Rightarrow R=U\,\mathrm{diag}(1,1,\det(UV^T))\,V^T$.

### Rotation averaging
Chordal L2 mean of quaternions (Markley): the eigenvector of the **largest** eigenvalue of $\sum_i q_iq_i^T$ (4×4). It's sign-invariant. Karcher (geodesic) mean: iterate $\bar R\leftarrow\bar R\exp\big(\tfrac1N\sum\log(\bar R^TR_i)\big)$.

## Pitfalls interviewers ask
1. `Quaterniond(w,x,y,z)` ctor vs `coeffs()` = (x,y,z,w) vs `Map<Quaterniond>` over a (x,y,z,w) buffer.
2. Hamilton (Eigen, ROS) vs JPL (some VIO papers): different multiplication order and sign.
3. Naive R→q divides by $w\approx0$ at 180°. Use Shepperd.
4. `acos` of a value just outside [-1,1] gives NaN. Clamp.
5. Averaging or interpolating Euler angles or raw quaternion components is wrong (wrap-around, double cover).
6. `AngleAxisd` with a non-unit axis silently gives a non-rotation.
7. Integrated R drifts off SO(3). Re-orthonormalize with SVD or renormalize the quaternion.

## Robotics angle
- Optimize on the tangent space: update $R\leftarrow R\exp(\delta)$ with 3 parameters, no constraint (right perturbation).
- IMU integration: $R_{k+1}=R_k\exp(\omega\,\Delta t)$, so you need the small-angle branch.
- Deskewing lidar: slerp the rotation between scan start and end per point timestamp.
- Multi-sensor calibration and pose averaging use the chordal mean, plus robust (Huber) weights in the tangent space.

## Rapid-fire questions
<details><summary>Show answers</summary>

- *DOF of a rotation? Params in R? Constraints?* 3; 9; 6 ($R^TR=I$), plus $\det=+1$.
- *Why quaternions over Euler?* No singularities, cheap composition, smooth interpolation.
- *Why quaternions over R?* 4 vs 9 numbers, easy renormalization, and they slerp.
- *Double cover?* $q$ and $-q$ represent the same rotation, because SU(2) → SO(3) is 2:1.
- *What does $p_w=R_{wb}p_b$ mean?* It expresses a body-frame point in world coordinates. The columns of $R_{wb}$ are the body axes written in world.
- *det of a reflection?* −1.
- *Right vs left perturbation?* $R\exp(\delta)$ perturbs in the body frame; $\exp(\delta)R$ perturbs in the world frame.
- *Why not average 4 quaternion components directly?* Sign ambiguity: $q$ and $-q$ cancel out.
